## Corrected research logic

The corrected manuscript direction is internally coherent in Sections 1 to 3:

1. Vehicle-level scheduling allows all FIFO-preserving interleavings of approach queues.
2. A platoon partition restricts this feasible sequence space by requiring vehicles inside each platoon to pass consecutively.
3. The restriction can only increase the optimal average delay relative to the unrestricted vehicle-level optimum, giving the nonnegative platooning-induced gap \(G(\Pi)=D^*_{\Pi}-D^*\ge 0\).
4. Section 3 first proves a partition-specific upper bound
   \[
   0 \le G(\Pi) \le \widehat{G}(\Pi).
   \]
5. It then derives a rule-level upper bound for partitions generated by a critical-headway and maximum-size rule:
   \[
   0 \le G(\Pi) \le \widehat{G}(\Pi) \le \widehat{G}(\delta,P_{\max}).
   \]
6. The role of the theory is to identify controllable factors in the gap: internal release-time gaps and the number of internal platoon links.
7. The proposed platoon-formation method should be deterministic preprocessing: join the current platoon only when the consecutive release-time headway is at most \(\delta\) and the current platoon size is below \(P_{\max}\).
8. Gurobi remains appropriate for downstream vehicle-level or platoon-level scheduling. It should not be used to optimize platoon boundaries for the proposed PP method.

This is a rule-guided preprocessing paper, not a partition-selection MILP paper.

## Section 3 theoretical checks

I reviewed `paper/sections/theoretical_analysis.tex` against `paper/sections/formulation.tex`, `notes/proof_development.tex`, `notes/formal_proof_audit.md`, and `notes/indexed_bound_derivation.md`.

### Partition-specific bound

The theorem states
\[
\widehat{G}(\Pi)
=
\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\Pi}}
\left[
\alpha_e[d_e-\hF]_+
-\frac{2(\hS-\hF)}{N}
\right]_+,
\qquad
\alpha_e=\frac{N-i_e}{N}.
\]

This matches the indexed proof notes. The proof uses the left-to-right global repair, the affected-vehicle count \(M_e\le N-i_e\), division by \(N\), and monotonicity of the positive-part operator. The inequality direction is correct:
\[
0\le G(\Pi)\le \widehat{G}(\Pi).
\]

### Rule-level transition

The corollary derives
\[
\widehat{G}(\delta,P_{\max})
=
\left(N-\left\lceil\frac{N}{P_{\max}}\right\rceil\right)
\left[
\frac{N-1}{N}[\delta-\hF]_+
-\frac{2(\hS-\hF)}{N}
\right]_+.
\]

The transition checks out under the stated rule:

- Notation is consistent with Section 2 and the Section 3 theorem: \(N\), \(\hF\), \(\hS\), \(d_e\), \(i_e\), \(\alpha_e\), \(\mathcal E_\Pi\), \(\Pi\), \(\delta\), and \(P_{\max}\) are used consistently.
- The inequality direction is correct because each internal-link contribution is upper-bounded and the number of internal links is also upper-bounded.
- The use of \(d_e\le \delta\) is valid for every internal link generated by the proposed rule, because the rule joins consecutive same-approach vehicles only when the consecutive release-time gap is at most \(\delta\).
- The use of \(\alpha_e\le (N-1)/N\) is correct because \(i_e\ge 1\).
- The link-count bound \(|\mathcal E_\Pi|\le N-\lceil N/P_{\max}\rceil\) is correct for any partition of \(N\) vehicles into platoons of size at most \(P_{\max}\), since at least \(\lceil N/P_{\max}\rceil\) platoons are required and \(|\mathcal E_\Pi|=N-K\).
- The explanation of controllable factors is accurate: \(\delta\) controls the per-link release-gap contribution, and \(P_{\max}\) controls the maximum possible number of internal links.
- Section 3 does not state that \(\widehat{G}(\delta,P_{\max})\) is used to optimize a partition.

No concrete mathematical error was found in the revised theorem or corollary. I did not change the theorem.

## Correct future comparison

The future computational story should compare only:

- NP: singleton platoons; vehicle-level reference optimum when solved.
- CHP: threshold-only critical-headway platooning using the same \(\delta\) as PP and no effective size cap.
- PP: threshold plus finite \(P_{\max}\), generated by the deterministic rule.

The intended claim is not that PP wins every metric. The intended claim is that PP improves the performance-computation trade-off by reducing excessive threshold-only loss while retaining meaningful dimension reduction and computational benefit relative to NP.
