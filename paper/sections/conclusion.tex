\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper establishes a theoretically controlled preprocessing framework for vehicle scheduling at general conflict areas. The FIFO-indexed bound quantifies the worst-case average-delay loss caused by a contiguous platoon partition without imposing a probabilistic arrival assumption. The bound then guides selection of an aggregation level consistent with the scheduler's model-size budget and acceptable performance loss.

Exhaustive small-instance calculations found no violation of the analytical guarantee and included equality cases. In the controlled scalability experiment, the proposed partition reduced the downstream ordering-variable count by approximately \(72\%\) to \(76\%\) for \(N=20,30,40\). The vehicle-level formulation proved optimality in only \(5\) of \(30\) instances at \(N=20\) and none at \(N=30\) or \(N=40\), whereas the reduced formulations retained substantially greater solvability under the same 30-second limit. These results demonstrate that the indexed bound is useful not only as a theoretical guarantee but also as a practical guide for reducing downstream MILP dimension.
