\section{Numerical Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments will distinguish theorem verification, bound usefulness, dimensional reduction, and downstream solver behavior.

\subsection{Exhaustive small-instance verification}

Small systems will use \(L\in\{2,3,4\}\) and small \(n_l\). For every instance, the study will enumerate all FIFO-feasible sequences and all contiguous platoon partitions to compute
\begin{equation}
D^*,\qquad D^*_{\Pi},\qquad G(\Pi),\qquad B_{\mathrm{idx}}(\Pi).
\end{equation}
The required validity check is
\begin{equation}
0\le G(\Pi)\le B_{\mathrm{idx}}(\Pi).
\end{equation}
The tightness measures are
\begin{align}
\text{Tightness ratio}&=\frac{G(\Pi)}{B_{\mathrm{idx}}(\Pi)},\\
\text{Bound excess}&=B_{\mathrm{idx}}(\Pi)-G(\Pi).
\end{align}
Cases with \(B_{\mathrm{idx}}(\Pi)=0\) will be reported separately.

\subsection{Dimension-reduction comparisons}

The following methods will be compared:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item vehicle-level scheduling without platooning;
  \item critical-headway platooning;
  \item fixed-threshold and fixed-size platooning; and
  \item the proposed bound-aware partition.
\end{enumerate}
Comparisons will control either the theoretical loss budget or the downstream model dimension.

\subsection{Scalability and traffic conditions}

The first completed scalability study fixes \(L=4\), balanced demand, Poisson arrivals, \(\hF=1\), \(\hS=2\), and a downstream time limit of \(30\) seconds with one Gurobi thread per model. Each size uses \(30\) independently generated instances. The completed sizes are \(N\in\{20,30,40\}\); the \(N=60\) case is reserved for later extension because the current three sizes already show the main computational transition.

The reported measures are the reduced-model ordering-variable count \(C(\Pi)\), end-to-end wall time \(T_{\mathrm{partition}}+T_{\mathrm{downstream}}\), branch-and-bound node count, fixed-time MIP gap, and incumbent objective value when the downstream model reaches the time limit. For \(N=20\), the vehicle-level model proves optimality in \(5\) of the \(30\) instances, while the reduced models solve \(359\) of \(360\) method-instance combinations to proven optimality. For \(N=30\), the vehicle-level model reaches the time limit in all \(30\) instances, but the reduced models still prove optimality in \(276\) of \(360\) method-instance combinations. For \(N=40\), the vehicle-level model again reaches the time limit in all \(30\) instances, and the reduced models prove optimality in \(105\) of \(360\) method-instance combinations.

The proposed bound-aware partition preserves a substantial dimension reduction across all three sizes. The mean ordering-variable counts are \(41.64\) at \(N=20\), \(88.78\) at \(N=30\), and \(141.26\) at \(N=40\), compared with vehicle-level counts \(150\), \(337\), and \(600\), respectively. This corresponds to mean dimension reductions \(72.24\%\), \(73.65\%\), and \(76.46\%\). The same runs show that fixed-size platooning typically produces the smallest reduced model, whereas the proposed method yields the smallest theoretical loss guarantee. At \(N=30\), for example, the mean indexed bounds are \(20.21\) for fixed size, \(6.06\) for the release-threshold baseline, and \(4.90\) for the proposed partition. At \(N=40\), the corresponding means are \(22.75\), \(7.76\), and \(5.46\).

These results support the paper's main computational claim: the indexed bound is useful not because it always minimizes the reduced model dimension, but because it selects partitions that remain much smaller than the vehicle-level model while providing substantially tighter deterministic loss guarantees.
